FFmpeg

Every download of Xorvio includes its own copies of FFmpeg's ffmpeg and ffprobe tools, named xorvio-ffmpeg and xorvio-ffprobe. Xorvio uses them to play formats its window can't play by itself, to pause and rewind live TV, to record, to cast to a TV, and to read what a video holds. They're built from the upstream source releases below, with only the parts Xorvio uses.

Built with x264, each copy as a whole is under the GNU GPL, version 2 or later, on macOS and Windows, and version 3 or later on Linux, where OpenSSL's Apache license joins it. Both fit Xorvio's own license. On macOS and Linux, FFmpeg uses the system's zlib, and on macOS and Windows, the system's own secure connections.

Every release on GitHub carries the exact source code of each of these beside its downloads, with notes on how each copy was built. Scripts/build-ffmpeg.sh in the release's source builds them again, and xorvio-ffmpeg -buildconf prints how any copy was configured.

If you've installed comskip, Xorvio uses it to find ad breaks in recordings. It's a separate GPL program that doesn't come with Xorvio.

Xorvio isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the FFmpeg project or VideoLAN.

Libraries in the app

These are the most significant. Each is under its own license, which its source carries. The full lists are Cargo.lock and package-lock.json in Xorvio's repository.

  • Tauri, which makes Xorvio a desktop app on every platform. Apache-2.0 or MIT.
  • React and React DOM, for the interface. MIT.
  • hls.js, which plays HLS streams. Apache-2.0.
  • mpegts.js, which plays MPEG-TS streams and the video FFmpeg repackages. Apache-2.0.
  • Zustand and TanStack Virtual, for the interface. MIT.
  • Lucide icons. ISC.
  • The Inter typeface, by Rasmus Andersson. SIL Open Font License 1.1.
  • SQLite, built in through rusqlite, which keeps the catalog and your library. SQLite is in the public domain; rusqlite is MIT.
  • Tokio, hyper and axum (MIT) and reqwest (MIT or Apache-2.0), for networking and the small media server Xorvio runs on your computer.
  • Rustls (Apache-2.0, ISC or MIT) and ring (Apache-2.0 and ISC), for secure connections, with Mozilla's root certificates through webpki-roots (CDLA-Permissive-2.0).
  • keyring, which keeps logins in your system's keychain. MIT or Apache-2.0.
  • mdns-sd, which finds TVs on your network. MIT or Apache-2.0.
  • RustCrypto's argon2 and chacha20poly1305, which seal logins in backups. MIT or Apache-2.0.
  • serde, chrono, quick-xml, flate2, zip, trash and many more, under the MIT or Apache-2.0 licenses.

Xorvio's windows are drawn by your system's own web view: WebKit on macOS, WebKitGTK on Linux, and Microsoft Edge WebView2 on Windows. They're part of your system, not of Xorvio.

Details and services

TMDB

When there's a TMDB key, film and series details, images, cast, trailers and people's pages come from The Movie Database (TMDB).

This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

TVmaze

Series information, including descriptions, cast and images, comes from TVmaze when TMDB can't help. It's licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA). Xorvio shows it with its formatting removed, and it stays under the same license.

Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons

Credits on people's pages, when TMDB can't help, come from Wikidata, which publishes its data under CC0. Their photos come from Wikimedia Commons, each under the license its page there gives.

IntroDB

The times of intros, recaps and end credits come from IntroDB, a free database timed by its community.

Trakt

Watch history sync, charts and lists come from Trakt, through its API. Xorvio isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Trakt.

Plex, Jellyfin and Emby

Xorvio connects to Plex, Jellyfin and Emby servers through their published interfaces. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Plex, the Jellyfin project or Emby.

Google Cast and YouTube

Casting uses Google Cast and Google's Default Media Receiver on your TV. Trailers play in YouTube's embedded player, under YouTube's terms of service. Xorvio isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Google.

Add-ons

Add-ons speak the Stremio add-on protocol, which Stremio publishes openly. Xorvio comes with no add-ons and isn't affiliated with Stremio.

Demo library

The demo library uses fictional titles. Some of its videos are open films by the Blender Foundation, Big Buck Bunny (© 2008 Blender Foundation, peach.blender.org) and Tears of Steel (© Blender Foundation, mango.blender.org), licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. The others are public test streams published for testing players by Apple, Unified Streaming, Mux and Test Videos.

The screenshots show Xorvio with a library that doesn't exist. Every title, channel and person in them is fictional, and all the artwork was made for this site.
